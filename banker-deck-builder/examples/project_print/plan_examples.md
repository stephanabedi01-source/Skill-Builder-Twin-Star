# Per-slide plan examples (as proposed to the user, before building)

These show the expected format: story job, takeaway, what goes where, numbers, flags, what moves out. Both were
approved and built with few or no changes.

---

## Example 1: Recovery Waterfall

**The story job.** Every slide so far showed that value is less than the debt. This slide shows exactly where it
runs out and who holds the value at that point: secured debt is paid in full, value breaks in the unsecured notes,
equity gets nothing, and the Holdings notes end up with about two-thirds of the value left for unsecured
creditors. That makes them the natural owners of the reorganized company and hands off to the Recommendation.

**Takeaway:** "Secured claims are covered in full, value breaks in the unsecured notes at 18c to 36c, and equity
receives nothing"

**Left panel (main chart): "Waterfall by Priority at Mid Plan Value ($M)"**
- One horizontal row per class in priority order; the claim as an outline bar with the recovered portion filled in
  (ink = paid in full, accent = partly paid, empty = nothing); columns for claim, recovery $, mid % and low to high.
- A red dashed line between the 2L notes and the Holdings notes: "Value breaks here: $947M left for $3,521M of
  unsecured claims".

**Right top: "Plan-Value Recovery vs. Market Price"**: per tranche, a range bar from low-EV to high-EV recovery, a
tick at mid, and a diamond at the bond price (TLB 100% vs 77c, 1L 100% vs 88c, 2L 100% vs 68c, Holdings 24% to 48%
vs 46c, legacy 12% to 24% vs 32c). The market agrees the unsecured notes are the fulcrum; the legacy notes trade
above their range, so they are the class most likely to object.

**Right bottom: "Who Holds the Fulcrum Value (Mid)"**: split of the $947M (Holdings $626M 66%, pension $212M 22%,
legacy $110M); callout on why Holdings recover about 2x (they claim twice against the operating company).

**Closing line:** "The Holdings notes are the fulcrum and hold two-thirds of unsecured value: they should lead the
restructuring and own the reorganized Xerox"

**Footnotes:** EV and claim basis; IPCo priority and residual; the double-dip; bond price date.
**Appendix:** full low/mid/high table by tranche; waterfall assumptions; optional toggle sensitivity on a model copy.
**Judge questions answered on the page:** why the 2L trades at 68c but recovers 100% (priming risk and duration:
reasons to support a fast prepack); why legacy trades above its range (holdout value; answered by cramdown).

## Example 2: Preliminary Recommendation (redo)

**What was wrong with the old slide:** a wall of text that still recommended the old structure; it did not pay off
the story. The conclusion should land the argument in one look.

**Takeaway (the conclusion):** "File an RSA-backed prepack that hands Xerox to the fulcrum notes, halves the debt
and clears every maturity to 2031"

**Left (main graphic): "Before and After the Prepack"**: four small native bar pairs, status quo accent vs. pro forma
ink: total debt $4.3B to $2.2B; net debt / 2027E EBITDA 5.0x to 2.2x; 2027E cash interest $363M to $189M;
maturities 2027 to 2030 $2.6B to $0. One line underneath: liquidity at 2030 from a $2.1B shortfall to $1.4B. This
answers the problem from slides 4 to 6.

**Right top: "Why the Holdings Notes Sign"**: recovery under each path: out-of-court exchange 20c, strict-priority
waterfall 36c, market price 46c, prepack 52c. Only the prepack beats where the notes trade today, which is why the
fulcrum signs and why the exchange is rejected.

**Right bottom: "Plan Terms by Class"**: secured extended for a 1% fee (100%); IPCo repaid by an exit term loan
(100%); Holdings 85% of equity + 10c takeback (52c); legacy 15% + 5c (26c); pension and trade unimpaired;
preferred and common cancelled. The 52c / 26c split preserves the waterfall's 2:1 ratio (defense against unfair
discrimination).

**Bottom strip: timeline**: sign RSA Dec '26; solicit Jan '27; file after the 2/17 premium step-down; emerge Q2 '27;
12+ months of cushion before the May '28 springer.

**Closing line:** "Value breaks in the unsecured notes, so the unsecured notes should own Xerox: the prepack pays
them above market and leaves a business that can carry its debt"

**What moves out:** alternatives considered, key considerations (tax, voting thresholds, TPG consent, ratings),
full pro forma tables: appendix or video. The old banner goes: the takeaway carries the conclusion.

**Judge question pre-answered:** legacy notes may vote no at 26c vs. a 32c market price; cramdown still works
because the Holdings class accepts, equity gets nothing, and 26c beats 7.9c in liquidation.
