# Storyline playbook: restructuring (RX) pitch decks, and how to adapt it

The Project Print deck argued "a solid, recurring franchise with a broken balance sheet". The arc below is the
general RX version; each slide proves one link of the chain and hands off to the next.

## The arc (12 main pages)
| # | Slide | What it must prove | Hand-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agenda | Order of the argument | |
| 2 | Company snapshot | The business is real, scaled, worth preserving | ...but look at the balance sheet |
| 3 | Situation overview | How it broke (capital allocation, cash quality), when the walls hit | who holds what |
| 4 | Capital structure | Priority, entities, guarantees, prices; where the market says value breaks | can the plan fix it? |
| 5 | Business plan | Normalized EBITDA is achievable but cannot carry the debt | so what is it worth? |
| 6 | Valuation summary | Value range vs. claims; equity out of the money; the fulcrum | the methods behind it |
| 7 | Trading comps | Peer multiples are a ceiling; the market already discounts | deals say the same |
| 8 | Precedents | Buyers de-rated the sector; comparable credits fixed it with prearranged deals | even crediting the plan... |
| 9 | DCF | Intrinsic value still short of the debt; no reasonable case covers it | what if it liquidated? |
| 10 | Liquidation | Liquidation destroys value; plan clears the best-interests test | so who gets the value? |
| 11 | Recovery waterfall | Exactly where value breaks; who holds the fulcrum value | so they should own it |
| 12 | Recommendation | The transaction, why the fulcrum signs, before/after, path and deadline | (conclusion) |

Main-page limits drive everything: if the required topics fill the main pages, no topic gets a second slide, and
industry overviews, stock charts, alternatives and key considerations go to the appendix or the video.

## Analytics a judge will probe (have the answer on the page or in a footnote)
- Fulcrum identification consistent across capital structure, valuation, waterfall and recommendation.
- EV vs. price per share: in RX, value is EV because claims are paid from EV; show implied equity as negative.
- Market-implied TEV (debt at market + equity + preferred - cash) vs. the valuation range.
- Different bases across slides (amortization vs. face; secured as drawn at filing vs. face; LTM vs. FY).
- WACC sanity: cost of equity above pre-tax cost of debt; reorganized-company target structure for plan value;
  size and company-specific premia flagged as estimates.
- Liquidation claims pool (trade, pension) and the best-interests test.
- Entity-level structure: double-dip claims, structurally senior JVs, guarantor vs. non-guarantor value.
- Why a filing now, given current liquidity: deadlines (springing maturities), document-driven dates (premium
  step-downs), tax (COD income exclusion, Sec. 382 (l)(5)/(l)(6)), speed of prepacks vs. free falls.
- Holdout and rejecting-class mechanics: voting thresholds (2/3 in amount, >1/2 in number), cramdown requirements,
  unfair-discrimination defense (preserve the waterfall's recovery ratio between classes).
- Recovery vs. market price by class: the proposal should beat the fulcrum's trading price.
- Feasibility of the reorganized company (liquidity, leverage, coverage, no maturities in the projection window).

## Adapting to other banker decks
The same machinery works for M&A pitches, IC memos in slide form, buy-side/sell-side materials and equity research
style decks: replace the arc (e.g. situation -> strategic rationale -> valuation -> process/timeline ->
recommendation), keep the per-slide discipline (claim takeaway, 2 to 4 exhibits, one accent, closing line, sourced
footnotes, render/QA loop). Ask the user for the required sections and page limits first.
