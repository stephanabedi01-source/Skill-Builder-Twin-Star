# Writing, numbers and footnotes

## Takeaways (the band under the title)
- One line (about 100 to 120 characters; check it renders on one line). A claim with numbers, not a topic.
  - Good: "Even at peer multiples of 5.9x to 6.4x, Xerox is worth $0.4B to $0.7B less than its $4.4B of debt"
  - Bad: "Public comparables analysis overview"
- Prefer claims that rest on observable facts (prices, filings) over calculated ones a judge can dispute.
- The recommendation slide's takeaway IS the conclusion of the deck.

## Closing lines (the dashed box at the bottom)
- One sentence: the conclusion of this slide + the bridge to the next. Pattern: "[finding]: [implication]".
  - "Peer multiples are a ceiling, not a target: even the peer median leaves Xerox $0.4B short of its debt..."
  - "Liquidation is the floor, not an option: it destroys ~$0.9B of value and cuts noteholder recoveries..."
- Bold the key number, accent the bad number, italic the implication phrase.

## Callouts
- One sentence, the "so what" of the adjacent exhibit, ideally pre-answering the obvious question
  ("Market cap is $324M but value is negative: each class of claims is paid from enterprise value...").
- Bold the numbers that matter; accent only the bad one.

## Voice
- No personal pronouns on slides: never "we / our / us / you / I / my". Rewrite: "The selected 5.0x to 6.0x sits
  below the peer band", "each class of claims is paid from enterprise value".
- No em dashes or en dashes as punctuation; use a colon, a comma, or "to" for ranges ("4.9x to 6.4x",
  "2028 to 2030"). A lone "–" in a table cell for nil is the banker convention and is fine.
- No AI phrasing (delve, seamless, robust, unlock, landscape, testament). No hedging filler.
- Short, declarative, specific. Every adjective earns its place or goes.

## Numbers
- Units written out on labels: "$1,739M", "$16.7B", "5.9x", "36%", "52c" (cents for recoveries and bond prices),
  "pp" for percentage points, "bps" for basis points, "mm" in footnotes when the source uses it.
- Negatives in parentheses with the sign outside: "($990)", "($152M)" (not "$(152)M").
- Ranges with "to": "$3.1B to $3.7B". Approximations with "~".
- Period on every figure: FY'25 vs. LTM 2Q'26 vs. 2027E. Never mix periods silently in one chart.
- Round consistently on a slide; make the visible pieces add up, or note "may not sum due to rounding".
- Tie out across slides: a number that appears on two slides must match, or a footnote must explain the different
  basis (e.g. maturities with amortization vs. face at final maturity; secured claims at face vs. as drawn at
  filing). When an upstream number changes, update every slide that quotes it in the same delivery.
- Use the exact source value for rounding (6.2514 shows as 6.3x, not 6.2x).

## Footnotes and sources
- Superscript markers placed exactly on the number or label they explain; one marker style (plain "1").
- Notes line: "Notes: (1) ...; (2) ..." compact, numbered in reading order; Sources line: "Sources: ..." naming
  filings, data vendor with date, and the model tab.
- Footnote every estimate, every non-obvious basis, every number that differs from another slide, every truncated
  bar, every paraphrased quote.
- Flag estimates explicitly in notes and in model comments ("Estimate.").

## Sourcing discipline
- Real, sourced numbers only. If a figure cannot be traced (e.g. "~26% of PF sales" with no source), do not use it;
  replace the text with a structural statement and tell the user.
- Quotes must be exact with a source, or footnoted as a paraphrase.
- Logos: use the user's own (copy them from their slides). Never fabricate logos.
- Check that the comp set and deal set are current: flag peers that have since been acquired, taken private or
  delisted (their multiples are no longer undisturbed trading multiples) and say how the median moves without them.
- Every data line needs an as-of date (e.g. "FactSet as of 10/14/26"); if the user did not give one, ask, and do not
  leave a placeholder in the final file.
